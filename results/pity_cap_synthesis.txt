SYNTHESIS: THE DISCRETE GOALPOST WIDENING EFFECT
======================================================================

  CRITICAL CONTEXT: A typical player stops around 150 expected points,
  which is reached at approximately roll 38 (cap=80) or roll 20
  (cap=10). The S1 pity cap at roll 80 is FAR BEYOND this natural
  stopping point.

  THE MONETISATION REALITY:

  At 400 RP per roll (optimal package combination):
    - Reaching 150 pts (roll ~38):  ~102.49
    - Reaching the pity cap (roll 80):   ~213.98
    - Gap (the sunk-cost trap):       ~111.49

  A player at 150 points has spent ~102.49. The pity guarantee
  is another ~111.49 away. This gap is the monetisation sweet
  spot: large enough to extract significant additional spend, but framed as
  "finishing what you started" rather than a new purchase.

  At the typical stopping roll (38) with cap=80, only 16.5% of
  players have triggered S1. The remaining 83.5% leave without ever
  experiencing the pity guarantee. The cap exists not as a practical
  mechanic but as a psychological anchor.

  THE UNCERTAINTY WINDOW AND SPENDING PRESSURE:

  At cap=10:  The uncertainty window is 0 rolls wide.
  At cap=80:  The uncertainty window is 79 rolls wide.

  This is a 79-roll expansion of the anxious zone. But the key
  insight is that this window overlaps almost perfectly with the typical
  player's entire session (rolls 1-38). The player spends their
  ENTIRE experience inside the zone of maximum outcome uncertainty.

  The pity cap at 80 rolls serves three psychological functions:

  1. SUNK COST ANCHOR: "I'm already 38 rolls in, and the guarantee is at
     80. I'm 42 rolls away from a sure thing." This reframes
     stopping as a loss rather than a natural endpoint. The gap represents
     ~111.49 of additional spend (optimal package combination).

  2. NEAR-MISS FRAMING: With only 16.5% triggering S1 by the typical
     stop, most players feel they "almost" got lucky. A lower cap would
     resolve this uncertainty earlier, reducing the emotional hook.

  3. DISCRETE INVISIBILITY: Each 10-roll cap increase adds only a modest
     number of anxious rolls (see marginal table above). No single change
     feels dramatic, but the cumulative effect from cap=10 to cap=80
     is substantial -- 79 extra anxious rolls worth
     £212.72 in potential spend.

  The interaction effects from the variance decomposition confirm that S1
  timing acts nearly independently of S2 and S3 (all interactions <1.5% of
  baseline variance). This means the pity cap is a clean, isolated lever --
  adjusting it does not create unpredictable knock-on effects. It is a
  precision tool for tuning spending pressure.
